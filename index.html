The Short Version

What Cursiv Actually Does

🔌

Runs On Your Machine

Cursiv talks to a local AI model (Ollama) that lives on your computer. No internet needed after the first install, no account required to use it, and nothing you type leaves your device unless you choose to add a cloud API key on top.

🧠

Remembers What You Teach It

Anchor a conversation and it's saved to your own private, searchable archive that later answers can draw on. Over time it builds a real record of what you've thought about and worked through — not somebody else's training data.

💬

Just Talk To It

Plain chat, not a command line. Ask a question, pull in a live web search when you need something current, dictate by voice, or generate an image — all from one window.

✉️

Something To Pass Down

Write private, sealed letters for the people who matter to you — opened only with the code you set for them. A family archive that outlasts any single conversation.

On the Guardrails

These Were Built to Protect You. Not to Watch You.

Cursiv has a Guardian firewall. A local-only auth system. A hard policy that your data never leaves your machine without your explicit action. Every one of those decisions was made for the same reason: to keep you safe from things that would compromise you — not to compromise you ourselves.

Guardian

The firewall faces outward

The Guardian layer was not built to monitor what you say or restrict what you can do. It was built to detect when something outside this system is trying to manipulate it — prompt injections, jailbreak attempts, unauthorized access. That lock faces away from you. Toward things that would use this system against you.

Auth

Local by design

Your login is bcrypt-hashed and stored on your machine. It is never sent to a server. The auth system exists because a tool with access to your files, your conversations, and your local AI should have a lock on the front door. That lock belongs to you — not to us, not to anyone else.

Data

No extraction. Ever.

No telemetry. No usage analytics. No conversation logging to any server. The update checker reads GitHub's public releases API — one outbound request to check a version number. Nothing about you, your prompts, your files, or your usage is collected. Nothing. There is no business model built on your data because there is no data leaving your machine.

Fleet

You control the network

The Fleet Dashboard only works if you set it up — you choose the relay, you choose the token, you choose who gets access. Machines only appear in your fleet if they have been explicitly authorized. This is not surveillance of your users. It is a tool for you to see your own machines.

Transparency

Be Honest With Yourself About What This Is

Speed

This is physics

Ollama runs on your CPU and GPU. Fast hardware = fast answers. On a modern gaming PC it's quick. On older hardware it's slower. That is not a limitation of the software.

Accuracy

Verify what matters

The model is not infallible. It will occasionally be wrong, confidently. Treat every answer as a starting point. Never use this to replace your own judgment. Ever.

Coding

70% is the goal

Designed to get you most of the way and force you to understand the rest. Feed the errors to a larger cloud model if you need to finish it. That's the point — not dependency, but understanding.

Privacy

Nothing leaves

Nothing you type is sent anywhere unless you add API keys, which you control. No telemetry. No usage logging. The update checker reads GitHub's public releases API — nothing about you is transmitted.

First Contact

The original concept — a personal AI workspace that didn't report to a cloud. Single agent, no council, no constitution. Just the question: what would it look like if this ran on your machine, followed your rules, and grew with your thinking? Everything since has been the answer to that question.

DECK 01

Mesopotamia

~3500 BCE – 500 BCE

The first cities. The first writing. The first laws. The Tigris and Euphrates gave birth to a model of civilization that everything since has been built on — or reacting against.

Cuneiform Hammurabi Ziggurats Trade Networks Sumer & Akkad

DECK 02

Egypt

~3100 BCE – 30 BCE

A civilization built on the certainty of death and the refusal to accept it. The Nile as spine. The pyramid as statement. Over 3,000 years of unbroken continuity that most modern nations can't come close to.

Pharaohs Hieroglyphics The Nile Afterlife Systems Engineering

DECK 03

Rome

~753 BCE – 476 CE

How a city on seven hills became the model for every republic and empire since. Its fall is more instructive than its rise — a lesson in what happens when institutions hollow out from the inside.

Republic → Empire Roman Law Infrastructure Military Strategy Fall & Legacy

DECK 04

China

~2100 BCE – 1912 CE

The oldest continuous civilization on earth. Four thousand years of dynasties, philosophy, invention, and a scale of social organization the West didn't encounter anything like until much later.

The Four Inventions Confucianism Silk Road Dynastic Cycles The Great Wall

DECK 05

Renaissance

~1300 – 1600 CE

Europe wakes up. The rediscovery of Greek and Roman thought, married to new money and a changed church, produced an explosion of art, science, and selfhood that we still live inside.

da Vinci Gutenberg Press Humanism Florence & Venice The Reformation

DECK 06

Industrial Revolution

~1760 – 1840 CE

The moment human civilization went from muscle power to machine power. Everything accelerated. Not all of it in the right direction. The gains were enormous; the cost was paid by people who had no say in the matter.

The transistor gave us computing. Computing gave us the internet. The internet gave everyone a voice — and then a microphone, an audience, an algorithm. We are still figuring out what we made.
